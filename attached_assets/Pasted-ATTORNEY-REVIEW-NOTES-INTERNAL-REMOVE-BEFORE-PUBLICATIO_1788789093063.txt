ATTORNEY REVIEW NOTES (INTERNAL — REMOVE BEFORE PUBLICATION)
This draft was prepared as a starting point and has not been reviewed by an attorney. Florida counsel review is requested on the following before publication:
	1.	Liability and indemnity (Sections 23–24): 12-month-fees cap; consequential-damage exclusions; indemnification scope and procedure; whether any carve-outs are required or advisable for gross negligence, willful misconduct, IP infringement, confidentiality breach, data/security incidents, or non-waivable statutory rights; enforceability of the "no personal liability" clause for LLC members/officers; and the one-year limitations period in Section 28.7.
	2.	Nationwide enforceability (Section 28): Florida governing law and Miami-Dade venue against out-of-state B2B customers; state business-protection statutes; state automatic-renewal laws (including California, New York, Illinois, District of Columbia, and others) and their disclosure, consent, acknowledgment, and renewal-reminder requirements, particularly for the 12-month Annual plan; ROSCA; electronic-contracting (ESIGN/UETA) sufficiency of clickwrap flow; prevailing-party fee clause.
	3.	PHI/HIPAA (Section 8): Adequacy of the no-PHI / no-BAA position given HHS guidance that a cloud provider receiving, maintaining, or transmitting ePHI may be a business associate regardless of encryption; whether the technical controls (warnings, pattern detection, ephemeral storage, no-content logging, AI-provider configuration) consistently support that position; exposure if a customer submits PHI anyway.
	4.	AI provider terms (Sections 6, 9.4, 18): Flow-down obligations from the AI provider's commercial terms and usage policy; accuracy of retention/training statements; Output ownership assignment language.
	5.	Privacy (Privacy Policy, separate document): CCPA/CPRA applicability to business-contact data; Washington My Health My Data Act and similar state health-data laws even in a no-PHI model; state breach-notification obligations; subprocessor disclosure.
	6.	Billing (Sections 13–15): Proration methodology; interest and collection-cost provisions against state usury limits; refund exceptions; chargeback language; sales-tax nexus and SaaS taxability by state.
	7.	Government customers (Section 26): Sufficiency of the carve-out; whether a separate public-entity addendum should be prepared.
	8.	Confidentiality (Section 16): Whether mutual confidentiality is commercially appropriate or should be one-way.
	9.	Future vendor/referral program: A separate reseller or referral agreement will be needed; Section 32.5 preserves Company's position that such parties cannot bind Company.
	10.	Consider for later, after counsel review only: mandatory arbitration, class-action waiver, and jury-trial waiver, which are intentionally omitted from this version.
